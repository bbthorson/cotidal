Three ingredients in equal measure, none of them hiding. Campari does the work:
bitter in a way that does not resolve, held in place by sweet vermouth that
gives it somewhere to sit rather than cancelling it out. The gin keeps the whole
thing upright.

It is the most-ordered bitter drink in the world and still the one people send
back. That is not a flaw in the recipe. A Negroni is an argument, and the
people who like it like being argued with — which is why it sits where it does
on the map, as far from comfort as the plane goes.

The equal-parts spec is a starting position, not a law. Lengthening the gin or
swapping the vermouth moves the drink without breaking it, and most bars you
trust are already doing one or the other.

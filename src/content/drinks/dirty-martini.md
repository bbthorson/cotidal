The Dirty Martini is the cocktail world's great aesthetic dividing line. Purists consider
it culinary vandalism—dumping cloudy, salty olive juice into delicate French vermouth and
juniper spirit. Devotees consider it the only drink that truly addresses hunger and fatigue.

The science is simple: salt suppresses perceived bitterness while heightening sweetness
and savory umami. In the glass, olive brine strips away the alcohol heat of the spirit,
making two and a half ounces of gin or vodka slip down like cold, savory broth.

Quality depends entirely on the brine. Jarred brine from industrial cocktail olives
tastes like metal and preservatives; brine decanted from high-grade Castelvetrano or
Spanish Manzanilla olives yields a luminous, silky drink tasting of crushed sea salt,
green fruit, and herbs.

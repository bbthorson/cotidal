Before October 1842, virtually all beer was dark, cloudy, or top-fermented. Pilsner
Urquell was the lightning strike that turned the drinking world towards clear, golden,
bottom-fermented lager.

Its balance is founded on contradictions. The water in Plzeň is astonishingly soft,
having filtered through silica-rich sandstone aquifers, which allows enormous quantities
of bitter Saaz hops to be boiled without imparting harsh or astringent tannins.

Triple-decoction mashing develops caramelization precursors that give the beer a round,
chewy malt sweetness reminiscent of water crackers and honeyed toast. Then the peppery,
grassy bitterness of Saaz cleans the palate instantly, leaving the mouth dry and
begging for the next swallow.

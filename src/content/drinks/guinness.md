Guinness is a triumph of sensory illusion: it looks like motor oil, pours like heavy
liquid cream, and drinks like light, refreshing black coffee.

Its deep color and bone-dry character come from unmalted barley roasted at 232°C until
charred. This roasted grain provides zero fermentable sugar, but delivers intense coffee-bean
bitterness, dark chocolate notes, and clean astringency.

The famous two-minute pour is not marketing theater; it is fluid mechanics. Nitrogen is
insoluble in liquid compared to carbon dioxide. Forced through five tiny holes in a tap's
restrictor plate, nitrogen breaks into millions of microscopic bubbles that cascade
downward along the glass walls, eventually rising to form an indestructible, dense white
dome of foam.

Underneath the thick head lies a surprisingly crisp, low-alcohol (4.2% ABV) dry stout
that leaves the mouth dry, refreshed, and ready for another pint.

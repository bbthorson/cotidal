The Mojito is an agricultural drink that suffers when treated like a laboratory sour.
Its soul is *hierba buena* (Cuban spearmint)—softer, more citrusy, and less menthol-forward
than common peppermint.

The central error in modern preparation is violent muddling. Grinding mint leaves against
the base of the glass tears plant veins and releases bitter, swampy chlorophyll. The mint
needs only to be pressed firmly into sugar and lime juice to rupture its surface oil glands.

Light, dry Cuban-style white rum brings clean alcohol and subtle molasses without
competing with the mint. Layered over cracked ice and lifted with soda, it is herbaceous,
sweet, and deeply cooling in high heat.

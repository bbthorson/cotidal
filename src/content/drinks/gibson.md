A single onion transforms the entire drink. Drop an olive into gin and vermouth, and
the result is salty, fruity oil. Drop a lemon peel, and you get bright floral citrus.
Drop a pickled cocktail onion, and the Martini pivots into the realm of the savory.

The onion brings mild allium pungency and pickled acetic acid to the glass. It binds
to the juniper, coriander, and angelica root of the gin, highlighting its dry, herbal,
and vegetal facets while keeping the vermouth taut.

A Gibson should be stirred colder and drier than a standard Martini. The pearl onion
rests at the bottom like a quiet pearl, absorbing gin and vermouth as you drink until
it becomes the ultimate savory finale.

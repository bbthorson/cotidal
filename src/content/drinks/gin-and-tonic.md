The simplest drink on this list and the one most often served badly. Everything
that can go wrong is a physical problem rather than a recipe problem: warm
glass, tired tonic, too little ice, gin poured over cubes that have already
started to melt. Ice does not dilute a drink faster when there is more of it —
it dilutes it slower, because the glass gets colder.

What makes it worth a page is the bitterness. Almost no other long drink brings
any, which is why the G&T reads as adult and refreshing at the same time and why
it lands on the savory side of the map despite the sugar.

The antimalarial story is true enough to be repeated and wrong enough to be
worth correcting. It is a drink that came out of a medicine cabinet, not a
prescription.

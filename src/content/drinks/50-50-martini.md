For decades, the mid-century American martini was a contest of machismo: who could make
it with the least vermouth, culminating in Churchill's joke about bowing in the direction
of France.

The 50/50 is the historical correction. Before Prohibition, vermouth was not treated as
a contaminant to be waved over the glass; it was celebrated as complex, botanical French
wine that softened juniper and brought floral herbs, coriander, and gentle acidity to
the table.

Audrey Saunders resurrected the 50/50 at Pegu Club to prove that good dry vermouth, kept
refrigerated and fresh, does not hide gin—it elevates it. Orange bitters provide the
bridge, tying the dry wormwood of the wine to the pine and citrus of the spirit.
Silky, herbaceous, and civilized.

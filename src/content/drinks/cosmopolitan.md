The Cosmopolitan is one of the most unjustly maligned drinks in cocktail history.
Its explosive popularity in the late 1990s led to millions of atrocious imitations:
sweet, fluorescent red goblets filled with sugary bar mix and cheap vodka.

Made according to Toby Cecchini's 1988 spec at The Odeon, however, the Cosmopolitan
is a brilliant modern daisy. It is structurally a citrus-vodka Sidecar or Margarita,
using Cointreau and fresh lime juice as its engine.

The cranberry juice should be used with strict restraint: just enough to tint the drink
a delicate, translucent sea-shell pink and provide a touch of tartness, never enough
to drown the spirit in red dye. Dale DeGroff's flamed orange peel on top adds smoky,
caramelized citrus oils that tie the whole architecture together.

Equal parts of four things, two of which are aggressively flavoured liqueurs.
On paper it reads like a dare. In the glass the Chartreuse's hundred-and-thirty
herbs and the maraschino's stone-fruit funk cancel each other's excesses and
leave something bright and strange that tastes like none of its parts.

It is also the clearest case on this list of provenance actually working. We
know the book, the year, the club, the man who carried it, the bartender who
found it again, and roughly the month it re-entered circulation. Compare that to
the Margarita, where four people claim the same drink and none of them wrote it
down.

The lesson generalises: cocktails have documented origins exactly when someone
had a commercial reason to print the recipe. Everything else is oral history.

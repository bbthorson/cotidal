The absinthe never really joins the drink. It coats the glass, is poured away,
and what remains is a smell arriving slightly before the whiskey does. That gap
— aroma first, then the rye, then Peychaud's on the finish — is the entire
point of the construction, and it is why the Sazerac is served without ice in a
glass that was chilled instead.

It is the closest thing American drinking has to a regional rite, and New
Orleans made it the official cocktail of the city in 2008. The lore around it is
correspondingly thick, and almost none of it survives contact with a date.

The Southside is often described as a 'Gin Mojito' or a 'Mint Gimlet', but its ancestry
stands firmly on its own feet.

The relationship between mint and gin is intensely complementary. Mint contains menthol
and essential herb oils that highlight the herbaceous botanical botanicals in London
Dry gin—cardamom, coriander, and orris root—while fresh citrus cuts cleanly across the palate.

Unlike the Mojito, where mint leaves remain in the highball glass with crushed ice, a
proper Southside is shaken with ice to extract mint oils and then double-strained through
a fine mesh strainer into a chilled coupe. This leaves the glass crystal-clear, free of
shredded green flecks, and crowned with a single fragrant slapped mint leaf.

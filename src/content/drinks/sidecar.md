Strong, sweet, sour: the Sidecar is the drink Embury used to argue about
proportion, and the argument is still live. His own ratio was eight parts
brandy to two of lemon and one of Cointreau — bracing to the point of austere.
The even-handed spec above is the modern consensus and is a good deal easier to
drink.

Cognac is what separates it from the rest of the family. Where a Margarita or a
Daiquiri gets its character from a bright, young spirit, the Sidecar has barrel
underneath the citrus, so it reads warm and dense despite being a shaken sour.
That is why it lands on the rich side of the map next to the after-dinner
drinks rather than beside its structural cousins.

The sugar rim is optional and the half-rim is a good compromise: it lets the
drinker choose per sip, which is more than most garnishes manage.

Before the Greyhound was swept up by vodka in the 1960s, it was a gin drink—and it was
vastly better for it.

Grapefruit juice is naturally bitter, acidic, and laden with citrus oils. Neutral vodka
merely dilutes and proofs the juice; London Dry gin actively collaborates with it.
The sharp pine of juniper, spicy coriander, and earthy angelica root in gin mesh
seamlessly with the bitter naringin of grapefruit pith.

Freshly squeezed grapefruit juice is essential. Canned or pasteurized juice lacks the
bright, stinging acidity and volatile peel aromatics needed to elevate the drink beyond
a breakfast beverage. Built simply over ice in a tall glass, it is dry, bitter, and
thirst-quenching.

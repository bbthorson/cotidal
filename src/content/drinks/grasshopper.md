The Grasshopper is unapologetic liquid confectionery. Stripped of whiskey seriousness
and bitter herbs, it aims directly for dessert nostalgia: melted mint-chip ice cream
with an alcoholic kick.

The equal-parts formula—green mint, white cacao, heavy cream—demands violent shaking.
Because liqueurs have high specific gravity and cream contains fat, shaking on hard ice
is necessary to whip the dairy into an airy, frothy emulsion that traps cold.

High-grade liqueurs make all the difference. Cheap commercial crème de menthe tastes
like toothpaste and chemical green dye; artisan French cordials (like Tempus Fugit) use
distilled botanicals and genuine cocoa beans, transforming an often-mocked supper club
novelty into a rich, cooling digestive masterpiece.

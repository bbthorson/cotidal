The Vieux Carré is New Orleans cocktail culture at its most decadent. While the Sazerac
is lean and ascetic—whiskey, sugar, bitters, and anise—the Vieux Carré is layered with
European luxury: French cognac, Italian vermouth, and medieval French herbal monastery
liqueur.

The brilliance lies in the split spirit base. Rye provides grain spice and tannic grip,
preventing the lush, grape-distilled cognac from becoming too unctuous. Bénédictine
acts as the sweetener in lieu of simple syrup, bringing saffron, angelica, and honeyed
spice into the heart of the drink.

The dual bitters are essential: Angostura brings earthy allspice and clove, while
Peychaud's introduces anise and bright cherry red fruit. It demands long, patient
stirring to soften its substantial alcohol and harmonize its dense botanicals.

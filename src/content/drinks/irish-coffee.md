A proper Irish Coffee is an architectural and thermodynamic triumph. It is not spiked
coffee stirred with milk; it is a warm, dark draught consumed entirely *through* a cold,
unsweetened blanket of cream.

The cream is the critical engineering challenge. If whipped too stiff, it blocks the
liquid; if unwhipped, it sinks into the coffee. Shaken briefly in a tin to ribbon stage,
heavy cream floats like a cloud on top of the dense, sugar-sweetened coffee.

Irish whiskey is uniquely suited here: light, triple-distilled, and fruity, with grain
sweetness that meshes with dark roast coffee without the heavy char of bourbon or the
smoke of Scotch. The hot, sweet liquid hits the palate simultaneously with the freezing,
unctuous cream.

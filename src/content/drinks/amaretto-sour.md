For decades, the Amaretto Sour was an embarrassing disco-era relic: cheap apricot-pit
liqueur drowned in fluorescent yellow sour mix from a soda gun.

Jeffrey Morgenthaler's 2012 recipe accomplished one of the greatest redemptions in modern
cocktail history. Morgenthaler diagnosed the fundamental flaw: amaretto is low in proof
(28% ABV) and high in sugar; mixing it with lemon juice alone creates a flabby, one-dimensional
sweet drink.

The solution was adding a three-quarter ounce of barrel-proof bourbon. The high-proof
grain whiskey supplies wood tannins, char, and alcoholic spine without obscuring the
amaretto's toasted marzipan notes. Fresh lemon provides authentic citric acid, while
shaken egg white creates a rich, creamy microfoam head that cushions the almond-bourbon
punch.

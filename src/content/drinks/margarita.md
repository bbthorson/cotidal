The Margarita's origin is a case study in how cocktail history actually
behaves. Four confident claims, all recorded decades later, all featuring a
woman the drink is supposedly named after — and underneath them a boring
structural explanation that fits the evidence: it is a tequila Daisy, and
daisies were a standing category long before anyone told a story about one.

Tommy's Style is here rather than the standard build because it is the version
that changed what the drink is for. Take out the orange liqueur and the sugar
stops being a flavour of its own; the agave nectar echoes the spirit instead of
competing with it, and a good tequila suddenly has somewhere to be heard. A bad
one has nowhere to hide, which is the trade.

Salt on half the rim, and only half. It is a seasoning, not a border.

Barolo is an austere wine masquerading as a delicate one. In the glass it is pale,
translucent garnet with an orange rim, looking almost fragile. On the palate it is
an iron fist: piercing acidity and immense, chalky tannins that seize the gums.

The Nebbiolo grape takes its name from *nebbia*, the autumn fog that blankets the
Langhe valleys during late October harvest. Its signature bouquet—often summarized
as 'tar and roses'—combines dark mineral soot, dried rose petals, leather, and sour
red fruit.

Barolo is unyielding when young. It requires either years of cellar aging to soften
its astringency into savory truffle and tobacco complexity, or hours in a decanter
alongside rich, fatty braised beef or white truffles to tame its fierce structure.

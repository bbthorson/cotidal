The Caipirinha is Brazil's rustic national monument. Unlike rum, which is almost always
distilled from cooked molasses byproducts, cachaça is fermented directly from freshly
pressed raw sugarcane juice. The resulting spirit is vegetal, grassy, funky, and pungent.

The drink's construction is deliberate chaos. An entire lime is cubed and crushed with
granulated sugar straight in the glass. The abrasive sugar crystals shred the oil glands
of the lime peel, extracting bitter, fragrant terpenes into the sour juice.

When fiery cachaça and crushed ice are stirred into this sludge, the sugar dissolves
unevenly, creating an evolving drink that moves from bright, puckering citrus to grassy
cane richness. It is loud, messy, and irreplaceable.

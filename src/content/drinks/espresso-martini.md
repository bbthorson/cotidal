A useful control case for everything else on this list. The Espresso Martini has
a known creator, a known city, a known decade, and a first-hand account of the
occasion, because it was invented recently enough that the man who made it lived
to be asked about it. Nothing older here is that well attested — which says less
about the drinks than about what survives.

It works because coffee is bitter and vodka is not anything, so the coffee has
the whole glass to itself. That also makes it unforgiving: stale espresso or
pre-brewed coffee from a jug is immediately obvious, and no amount of liqueur
covers it.

It sits rich and sweet on the map, but it is a good deal drier than its
reputation. Most versions ordered are oversweetened; the syrup is optional for a
reason.

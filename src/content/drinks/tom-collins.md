The Tom Collins gave its name to an entire category of glassware: tall, straight-sided,
and built for sparkling dilution. It is fundamentally a gin sour lengthened with cold
soda water, transforming an intense citrus-and-spirit shake into an afternoon thirst
quencher.

The traditional spirit is Old Tom gin—the lightly sweetened, rounder English style that
bridged eighteenth-century Dutch Genever and modern London Dry. Its malty botanicals
soften the sharp citric acid of fresh lemon without demanding excess sugar.

Long effervescence requires ice-cold soda and minimal stirring. A gentle lift of the
barspoon distributes the sweet-sour base up the height of the column without knocking
the bubbles out of suspension.

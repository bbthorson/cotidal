The Gimlet is not simply a gin sour made with lime. A gin sour uses fresh, cloudy lime
juice and simple syrup; a proper Gimlet demands lime cordial—a clarified preparation of
lime juice, lime peel oils, sugar, and citric/tartaric acid.

That distinction is everything. Cordial gives the Gimlet its characteristic crystalline
clarity and an intense, slightly candied lime-peel astringency that cuts cleanly through
heavy juniper gin.

Raymond Chandler's equal-parts ratio from *The Long Goodbye* is far too sweet with modern
corn-syrup Rose's cordial. Modern bartenders pull the cordial back to a 2:1 or 3:1 ratio,
or craft their own fresh lime oleo-saccharum cordial to capture the razor-sharp,
medicinal balance that once kept the British fleet alive.

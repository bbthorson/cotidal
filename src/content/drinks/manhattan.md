The Manhattan is where modern mixed drinking crystallized. Before it, mixed drinks
were variations on the ancestral formula: spirit, sugar, water, and bitters. By
swapping simple syrup for vermouth, the Manhattan introduced fortified, aromatized
wine into cocktail construction, trading pure sweetness for herbal complexity, acidity,
and depth.

Rye is the historic backbone. Its dry pepper cuts through the botanical weight of Italian
vermouth; bourbon softens the drink and turns it rounder and sweeter. The 2:1 ratio is
the benchmark, but vermouth choice dictates the balance: a rich, vanilla-forward vermouth
requires a high-proof rye to keep the drink from slouching.

Stirred, never shaken. Shaking breaks the texture, introduces cloudiness, and over-aerates
what is intended to be a silky, luminous pour.

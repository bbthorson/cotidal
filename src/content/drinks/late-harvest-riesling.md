Late-Harvest Riesling (*Spätlese* or *Auslese*) is an exercise in patient risk. Leaving
ripe grapes hanging on the vine into November risks devastating autumn freezes, hail,
and rot.

When autumn conditions cooperate, the reward is pure nectar. The grape clusters shrivel
under cold sun and gentle breezes, concentrating natural sugars while absorbing golden
autumn light.

What makes Riesling singular among sweet grapes is that its acidity never drops off a
cliff. Even at high sugar levels, the wine retains electric, mouth-watering tartness.
On the palate, it delivers a tidal wave of ripe peach, candied lemon peel, baked apple,
wild honeysuckle, and petrol-slate minerality, finished with a crisp, dry snap.
Pair with roasted duck, glazed ham, or fruit crumbles.

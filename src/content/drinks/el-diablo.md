Before the Margarita conquered the world, Trader Vic was quietly mixing tequila with
French blackcurrant liqueur in Oakland, California. The 'Mexican El Diablo' was his
nod to agave in the 1940s, pairing reposado tequila's earthy pepper with Burgundian
crème de cassis.

The drink's success hinges on the unexpected harmony between dark fruit and spicy ginger.
Crème de cassis brings deep blackberry and blackcurrant jam notes with rich tannins,
which are immediately brightened by fresh lime juice and electrified by carbonated
ginger beer.

The oak and caramel of reposado tequila anchor the highball, keeping it from descending
into a sugary fruit punch. It is complex, colorful, and dangerously crushable.

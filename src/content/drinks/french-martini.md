The French Martini was the vanguard of the 1990s 'tini' movement: cocktails that had
nothing to do with vermouth or gin, but were served in v-shaped stemware and celebrated
flavor, color, and texture.

The drink's technical virtue is the interaction between pineapple juice and Chambord.
When shaken violently against ice, the bromelain enzymes in pineapple juice create a
thick, creamy white head of froth (the 'crema') that sits atop the deep purple-pink liquid.

The rich, vanilla-kissed black raspberry and cognac notes of Chambord provide sweetness,
while the acidity of pineapple juice cuts the sugar. Vodka acts as the neutral propellant,
keeping the drink light, approachable, and effusive.

The Aperol Spritz owes its global ubiquity to brilliant, structural engineering: low
proof, high effervescence, and high visual contrast. In a large goblet crowded with
cubes of ice, its neon orange color operates as an open invitation across a crowded
terrace.

The formula is defined by the 3-2-1 ratio. Prosecco must be poured before the bitter
liqueur; pouring denser Aperol on top encourages natural mixing without aggressive
stirring, which preserves the delicate carbonation of the sparkling wine.

While Aperol is sweeter and lower in alcohol (11% ABV) than Campari, its bitter backbone
of gentian, rhubarb, and cinchona provides genuine digestive tension. Dry Brut Prosecco
is mandatory; sweet sparkling wine collapses the balance into syrup.

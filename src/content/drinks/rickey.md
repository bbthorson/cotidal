The Rickey is the anti-Collins. While most nineteenth-century highballs depended on
sugar or sweet cordials to make spirits palatable, Colonel Joe Rickey and the bartenders
at Shoomaker's eliminated sugar entirely.

The architecture is austere: juniper-heavy dry gin, cold carbonated water, and the
acid and aromatic skin oils of half a freshly squeezed lime dropped straight into the glass.
Because there is no sweetener to mask flaws or coat the tongue, the botanical clarity of
the gin and the quality of the carbonation are laid bare.

It is bracing, tart, and intensely refreshing. In high heat or humidity, the absolute
dryness quenches thirst in a way no sweetened citrus soda or sour can match.

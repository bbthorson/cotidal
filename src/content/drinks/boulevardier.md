The Boulevardier takes the architecture of the Negroni and anchors it in American
whiskey. Where gin lets Campari's gentian and cochineal sing with high, botanical
clarity, whiskey meets the bitterness with vanilla, oak, and corn sweetness.

The tension in the glass depends entirely on the base spirit. Bourbon yields a rich,
velvety drink where caramel and orange peel round out Campari's bite. Rye pushes the
spices forward—clove, black pepper, and bitter herbs—resulting in a tauter, more savory
profile.

Equal parts works when the whiskey is bold, but bumping the whiskey slightly allows
the grain to speak before the bitter finish closes the door.

The Vesper is an enormous drink: four and a half ounces of high-proof spirits before
dilution, designed for espionage tension in a French casino.

Its architecture is unique. Vodka softens the intense pine-needle hit of London Dry
gin, creating a silkier texture while maintaining ruthless proof. The bitter quinine
and orange peel of Kina Lillet—replaced in modern bars by Cocchi Americano since
Lillet dropped its quinine in 1986—provides a drying, medicinal bite that standard
dry vermouth cannot replicate.

Bond insisted on shaking: 'shaken very well until it's ice-cold'. While shaking bruises
vermouth and clouds gin, in the Vesper it produces micro-ice crystals and intense, rapid
chilling that takes the sting out of four ounces of raw spirit.

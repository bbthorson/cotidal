Gose is German brewing at its most anarchic. While Bavarian lagers are famously sterile
and restrained by the Reinheitsgebot, Gose—native to the medieval mining towns of the
Harz mountains and Leipzig—embraces wild bacteria, spice, and salt.

The grain bill is over half malted wheat, creating a hazy, pale yellow, bready foundation.
Fermentation with *Lactobacillus* produces clean, refreshing lactic acid (the bright sourness
of fresh yogurt), while crushed coriander seeds release citrusy terpenes and herbal spice.

The masterstroke is sea salt. Originally entering through saline groundwater in Goslar,
the salt rounds out the sharp sour acid, increasing salinity and body on the mid-palate
while leaving the tongue dry and craving another swallow. It is tangy, mineral, sparkling,
and undeniably thirst-quenching.

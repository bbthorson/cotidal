The Brandy Alexander is one of the rare instances where a variation permanently eclipsed
the original drink. While the original Alexander used gin, swapping juniper spirit for
oak-aged French brandy proved to be a stroke of culinary genius.

Cognac brings dark grapes, vanilla, toasted wood, and dried fruit, which meld seamlessly
with the roasted chocolate tones of dark crème de cacao and the rich fat of heavy cream.

The freshly grated nutmeg on the surface is not garnish; it is an active ingredient.
Its warm, pungent aromatic oil cuts through the dense dairy fat as the glass approaches
the nose, providing holiday spice and keeping the drink from tasting like melted chocolate syrup.

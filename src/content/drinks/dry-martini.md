A Martini is a temperature as much as a recipe. Cold enough and the gin's
botanicals tighten into something almost mineral; a few degrees warm and the
alcohol arrives naked and early. Nothing else in the drink is available to
cover for it.

The century-long march toward drier is worth knowing about, because it means
the drink most people picture — gin, barely acknowledged vermouth, olive — is a
late and fairly extreme position in a long argument, not the starting point. The
older ratios are not a compromise. They are a different and more forgiving drink
that happens to share the name.

It sits at the far crisp, far savory corner of the map, which is the honest
description of what it is: dry, exact, and unwilling to meet anyone halfway.

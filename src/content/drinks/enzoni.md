Vincenzo Errico's Enzoni was one of the earliest modern hits born from Sasha Petraske's
Milk & Honey.

The recipe is brilliant in its simplicity: a classic gin sour, but with an ounce of
Campari replacing half the gin, and five fresh green grapes muddled into the shaker.
Green grapes contain malic acid and delicate skin tannins; when crushed and shaken,
they lend a soft, vinous texture and pale jade translucence that binds Campari's intense
bitterness to the pine of London Dry gin.

Fresh lemon provides sharp citric snap, while simple syrup softens the edges. Served
over a single large rock of ice, it is fruity, bitter, refreshing, and deeply sophisticated.

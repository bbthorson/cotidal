The Martinez is the missing link in American cocktail evolution. If the Manhattan
proved that Italian vermouth belonged in grain spirits, the Martinez showed what
happened when vermouth met juniper.

It was not originally dry. Nineteenth-century gin was malty, lightly sweetened Old Tom,
and the vermouth of choice was rich and red. Maraschino liqueur was added not as fruit
flavoring, but as an aromatic modifier, bringing nutty cherry-stone funk and dry
sweetness to round out the botanical edges.

The resulting drink is amber, viscous, and deeply aromatic—far closer to an herbal
Manhattan than the bone-dry, crystal-clear Martini that would eventually bear its name.
Stirred gently to preserve its velvety weight.

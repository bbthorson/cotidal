Sancerre is the global yardstick for Sauvignon Blanc. Situated on steep, chalky hills
rising above the Loire River in central France, the appellation strips Sauvignon Blanc
of the tropical passionfruit and sweet guava common in warmer New World climates,
honing the grape down to chalk, flint, and bone-dry acid.

The hallmark is tension. On *silex* (flint) soils, the wine carries a smoky, gunflint
aroma (*pierre à fusil*) that smells like two rocks struck together under water. On
*terres blanches*, the same Kimmeridgian limestone found in Chablis delivers rounder
body balanced by laser-like lemon and grapefruit pith acidity.

Vinified almost universally in temperature-controlled stainless steel or neutral enamel
vats without oak, Sancerre is bottled young to preserve its electric energy. It is the
definitive companion for fresh goat's milk cheese (*Crottin de Chavignol*), oysters,
and summer heat.

The Pink Lady suffered a steep fall from grace in the post-war era, degraded by
industrial corn syrup grenadine and low-proof gin into a saccharine confection.

In its authentic 1911 form, however, it is an exquisite sour. The crucial ingredient is
American applejack or French Calvados. The apple brandy cuts through the juniper bite of
dry gin, providing orchard depth, barrel oak, and malic acid.

Real pomegranate grenadine—cooked from pure pomegranate juice and sugar—provides ruby
color and drying fruit tannins rather than cloying sweetness. Shaken vigorously with egg
white, it yields a powdery, rose-tinted microfoam that sits like silk on the tongue.

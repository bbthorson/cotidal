The Penicillin achieved modern classic status because it solved an old problem: how to
use heavily peated Scotch in a mixed drink without turning the glass into an ash heap.

The solution is structural separation. Blended Scotch forms the engine—malty, round,
and polite—blending seamlessly with the hot prickle of fresh ginger juice, wild honey,
and tart lemon. The peated Islay malt is restricted to the top of the glass.

As you drink, your nose meets campfire smoke, creosote, and sea iodine, but the liquid
meeting your tongue is spicy, sweet, and bright. It is medicine in the traditional,
restorative sense of the word.

The Bloody Mary is a savory drink in the full sense: tomato, salt,
Worcestershire's anchovy and tamarind, pepper. Nearly everything in it pulls
toward the savory edge of the map, and it sits there with the dirtiest
Martinis.

It is crisper than the tomato makes it sound. The lemon is the backbone and the
vodka adds nothing to soften it, so the drink reads sharp and bright rather than
thick — provided it is stirred, not shaken, and the tomato juice is cold.

Bars build it a dozen ways. Swap the vodka for tequila and it is a Bloody
Maria; for gin, a Red Snapper. On the map they all land near here: the tomato
and the seasoning outweigh the spirit.

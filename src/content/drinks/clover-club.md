Before the mid-century relegated pink cocktails to novelty status, the Clover Club
was the drink of Gilded Age Philadelphia power brokers.

The secret to its survival is the dry vermouth. Without vermouth, gin, raspberry, and
lemon produce a one-dimensional fruit tart. The addition of French dry vermouth introduces
herbal wormwood, wine acidity, and botanical dryness that reins in the rich berry sugars.

Fresh raspberry syrup—made by macerating fresh berries in sugar rather than using artificial
grenadine—supplies bright tartness and a pale, luminous rose hue. Shaken with egg white,
it pours with a dense, silken meringue cap that traps raspberry aromatics against the palate.

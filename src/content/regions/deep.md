The Deep is the quarter of the chart where rich meets savory: away from sugar,
toward weight. Nothing about it is a style or a tradition. It is a place on a
map, and the drinks in it are here because that is where the chart sets them.

What they turn out to have in common is old. Most of the cocktails here are
stirred, not shaken, and most stand on one of two frames. The older is the
cocktail as a newspaper defined it in 1806 — spirit, sugar, water, bitters —
which is still the [Old Fashioned](/drinks/old-fashioned) and the
[Sazerac](/drinks/sazerac). The other puts an aromatised wine beside the spirit:
whiskey and sweet vermouth, the [Manhattan](/drinks/manhattan), in print by
1884. The [Rob Roy](/drinks/rob-roy), the [Martinez](/drinks/martinez) and the
[Bijou](/drinks/bijou) are that frame with the spirit changed or a third bottle
added; the [Negroni](/drinks/negroni) and the
[Boulevardier](/drinks/boulevardier) arrive at it from the bitter aperitivo.

Not everything here is mixed. The chart places wines, beers and spirits as well
as cocktails, so [Barolo](/drinks/barolo), [Guinness](/drinks/guinness),
[Islay Scotch](/drinks/islay-scotch) and [Fernet-Branca](/drinks/fernet-branca)
sit in the same water as the Manhattan.
